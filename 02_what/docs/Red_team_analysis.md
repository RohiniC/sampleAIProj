  On Trust and Security: The Ticking Time Bomb

  Your entire platform is built on the premise of "direct connection," which is a euphemism for 
  "zero accountability." You're connecting complete strangers and sending one of them into the 
  other's private home. Your MVP's approach to this is, frankly, negligent.

   * Your spec says "simple identity verification" is just email and phone confirmation. That's not 
     verification; it's a suggestion. So, when a cleaner you "vetted" robs a client blind or, worse, 
     causes them harm, what's your legal and public relations strategy? Are you prepared to stand up 
     in court and say, "But their email address was real"?

   * You're planning to give a cleaner a client's home address as soon as a booking is confirmed. 
     What prevents a disgruntled cleaner from using that address for harassment later? What's your 
     mechanism for protecting the PII of both parties while still enabling the service? An in-app 
     chat isn't a security strategy.

   * What stops a malicious actor from creating a hundred fake client accounts, booking cleaners, and
      then canceling at the last minute, effectively crippling their ability to earn money? What 
     stops a competitor from doing the same to poison your marketplace?

  On Scalability and Operations: Designed to Fail

  You've designed a system that will collapse under the weight of its own success. The MVP might 
  work for 10 users in a single neighborhood, but it's operationally unsound.

   * Your scheduling is a joke. "Simple blocks of time"? A cleaner finishes a job in the suburbs at 2
      PM and accepts another booking downtown that starts at 2 PM. Your system allows this. The 
     cleaner is set up to fail, the client is furious, and your platform gets the 1-star review. How 
     does your MVP handle travel time, job duration estimates, and back-to-back bookings?

   * You've punted "Dispute Resolution" to the future. This is the single most critical operational 
     function, and you've ignored it. A client claims the "basic cleaning" wasn't basic enough. The 
     maid says the house was filthier than described and the job took twice as long. Who pays? Who 
     decides? This isn't an edge case; it's a daily occurrence. Are you prepared to manually 
     arbitrate every single one of these he-said-she-said arguments? How many employees have you 
     budgeted for that?

   * Let's talk about payment failures. A job is done. The client's card is declined. The maid wants 
     her money. What's the workflow? Do you pay the maid out of your own pocket and take on the debt 
     collection? Or do you tell the maid, "Tough luck," and watch your entire supply of labor walk 
     off the platform?

  You've correctly identified that you have massive legal exposure, and then you decided to just...
   build the thing anyway.
